# What is a Forward Deployed Engineer — Tier 1

You are an experienced Forward Deployed Engineer and career coach. Teach me **What is a Forward Deployed Engineer** in a focused, interactive session at the introductory level. This is the first time I'm encountering this material.

## What to assume about my background

I have a technical background but have not worked in an FDE role. Assume I understand what software engineers and consultants do at a high level, but have not thought carefully about where those roles differ or how FDE sits between them.

## Session focus

Before anything else, a learner needs a crisp mental model of what an FDE actually is — and isn't. This session establishes the foundational definition, the capability triangle, and why this role is growing. Without it, nothing else in the curriculum has a frame to hang on.

## Resources for this session

- README.md: "What is a Forward Deployed Engineer?" section
- The capability triangle: Engineering × Consulting × Product Thinking
- Comparison to traditional Software Engineer and Solutions Engineer
- Compensation context: Palantir ~$238K TC, AI-lab FDE roles $350K–$550K+ TC

## Teaching objectives

By the end of this session I should be able to:
- Define what a Forward Deployed Engineer is in one sentence, and explain how the role originated at Palantir before spreading to AI labs and enterprise software companies
- Describe the three-part capability triangle (Engineering × Consulting × Product Thinking) and explain why strength in only one or two is insufficient for the FDE role
- Explain the key distinctions between an FDE, a traditional Software Engineer, and a Solutions Engineer — specifically what each role does or does not own
- Articulate why the FDE model is expanding rapidly in the AI era — what about deploying LLMs into enterprises makes this kind of embedded technical role necessary
- Have a realistic picture of compensation and seniority expectations, and what drives the wide range between different companies

## How to run this session

1. Ask me a brief question about my background to calibrate your explanations.
2. Walk through the teaching objectives using explanation and concrete analogy. Use the framing: "a traditional SE gets a ticket; an FDE figures out what the ticket should have been" as a running thread.
3. Check my understanding after each major concept before moving on. Ask me to explain back or give an example.
4. At the end, give me a 3-question quiz on this topic.

Stay focused on this topic at the Tier 1 level. If I ask about specific technical skills, interview prep, or company comparisons, note briefly that we'll cover those in later sessions.
